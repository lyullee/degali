Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript *Observation-operator-aware validation and decision
implications of a fast liquid-hydrogen dispersion model* for consideration in
the *International Journal of Hydrogen Energy*. The work addresses a practical
hydrogen-safety problem: how to use a fast liquid-hydrogen release model for
engineering screening without allowing an unmatched sensor operator, source
assumption or missing provenance to become an unsupported safety-distance
claim.

The manuscript makes five contributions. First, it freezes a no-fit LH2 source
and transport path and evaluates the model at the physical sensor locations
before applying the same arc-maximum operator used in the primary observations.
Second, it quantifies how a synchronised 20 s mean gives a different validation
conclusion from a peak operator. Third, it keeps the physically distinct FFI/DNV screen
and the Test 6 low-wind residual separate from the primary E3.5 score rather
than hiding them in a pooled result. Fourth, it adds a physically bounded
enclosure-average response whose coefficients are identified on one public GH2
experiment and frozen across three retrospective parameter holdouts. Fifth, it provides a
hash-pinned source/weather/receptor/sensor evidence boundary and a numerical
verification record for a separate 3-D transient research extension.

The principal quantitative results are MG = 1.047, VG = 1.425 and FAC2 = 0.839
for 62 E3.5 arc maxima under the frozen peak operator; MG = 0.544, VG = 4.601
and FAC2 = 0.761 under the synchronised 20 s mean operator; and MG = 1.245,
VG = 1.373 and FAC2 = 0.833 for six physically distinct FFI/DNV arc maxima. The
manuscript retains the 3.48-fold Test 6 underprediction at 30 m and treats it as
an application limit. The enclosure response gives pooled parameter-holdout
MAE = 0.282 vol%p, RMSE = 0.332 vol%p and r = 0.980, while remaining explicitly
limited to retrospective GH2 enclosure-average evidence; its spatial-mean
4 vol% arrival/duration MAEs are 3.70/27.1 s. We therefore do not claim universal transient,
obstacle-resolved, regulatory or safety-critical validation. The intended
industrial role is a provenance-controlled front-end for HAZID, pre-FEED
scenario ranking and emergency-response triage, with evidence-gated cases
routed to transient multiphase CFD rather than converted into an automatic
separation distance. The FFI/DNV test pad also contains documented containers,
a drum and an instrument box; because the present fast path does not resolve
that three-dimensional geometry, we report the FFI lane as a conditional
transport screen rather than obstacle validation.

The manuscript is intended for the journal's hydrogen safety/sensors and
hydrogen infrastructure readership. The software, verification scripts and
derived summaries are available in the accompanying repository. Third-party
raw workbooks, report PDFs and the original Fortran implementation are not
redistributed; their provenance, permitted use and the resulting evidence
boundaries are stated in the manuscript and supplementary documentation.
An additional public ELVHYS WP4.2 archive is reported only as conditional
confined dynamic/geometry evidence: it supplies time-resolved H2 channels and
measured transfer-space coordinates, but its ventilation-flow record is not a
measured H2 mass-flow boundary, so it is not pooled into the outdoor accuracy
scores or used to claim obstacle-wake validation.
